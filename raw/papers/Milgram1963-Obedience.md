# Behavioral Study of Obedience

**Author**: Stanley Milgram (Yale University)  
**Journal**: The Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, Vol. 67, No. 4, 1963, pp. 371-378.  
**DOI**: 10.1037/h0040525  

---

## Abstract
This article describes a procedure for the study of destructive obedience in the laboratory. It consists of ordering a naive S to administer increasingly more severe punishment to a victim in the context of a learning experiment. Punishment is administered by means of a shock generator with 30 graded switches ranging from Slight Shock to Danger: Severe Shock. The victim is a confederate of the E. The primary dependent variable is the maximum shock that S is willing to administer before he refuses to continue further. 26 Ss obeyed the experimental commands fully, and administered the highest shock on the generator. 14 Ss broke off the experiment at some point after the victim protested and refused to provide further answers. The procedure created extreme levels of nervous tension in some Ss. Profuse sweating, trembling, and stuttering were typical expressions of this emotional disturbance. One unexpected sign of tension—yet to be explained—was the regular occurrence of nervous laughter, which in some Ss developed into uncontrollable seizures. The variety of interesting behavioral dynamics observed in the experiment, the reality of the situation for the S, and the possibility of parametric variation within the framework of the procedure, point to the fruitfulness of further study.

## Full Text Content Summary & Key Passages

### Introduction
Obedience is as basic an element in the structure of social life as one can point to. Some system of authority is a requirement of all communal living, and it is only the man dwelling in isolation who is not forced to respond, through defiance or submission, to the commands of others. From 1933-45 millions of innocent persons were systematically slaughtered on command in gas chambers and concentration camps. These policies could only be carried out on a massive scale if a very large number of persons obeyed orders.

### Method
- **Subjects**: 40 males, aged 20 to 50, drawn from New Haven and surrounding communities via newspaper advertisement and direct mail. Occupations: 37.5% skilled/unskilled workers, 40% sales/business/white-collar, 22.5% professionals. Paid $4.50 simply for showing up.
- **Personnel & Locale**: Conducted in the interaction laboratory at Yale University. Experimenter: 31-year-old biology teacher, stern, impassive, wearing a gray technician's coat. Victim (Learner): 47-year-old accountant of Irish-American stock, mild-mannered and likable.
- **Cover Story & Drawing**: Purportedly studying "the effect of punishment on memory and learning". The drawing was rigged so the naive subject was always the Teacher and the accomplice always the Learner.
- **Apparatus**: Shock Generator with 30 lever switches in a horizontal line, 15 to 450 volts, in 15-volt increments. Verbal labels: Slight Shock, Moderate Shock, Strong Shock, Very Strong Shock, Intense Shock, Extreme Intensity Shock, Danger: Severe Shock, XXX (435V, 450V). Sample shock given to the teacher: 45 volts.
- **Standardized Prods**:
  - Prod 1: Please continue. or Please go on.
  - Prod 2: The experiment requires that you continue.
  - Prod 3: It is absolutely essential that you continue.
  - Prod 4: You have no other choice, you must go on.
  - Special prods for injury/tissue damage and refusal.
- **Learner Feedback**: Pre-scheduled answers (approx 3 wrong to 1 right). No protest until 300V. At 300V, learner pounds on the wall and stops answering. Pounding repeated at 315V; thereafter silent.

### Results
- **Prior Predictions**: 14 Yale senior psychology majors predicted only 0% to 3% (mean 1.2%) would administer 450 volts.
- **Actual Distribution**:
  - 100% (40/40) continued past 300 volts.
  - 5 broke off at 300V.
  - 4 broke off at 315V.
  - 2 broke off at 330V.
  - 1 broke off at 345V, 1 at 360V, 1 at 375V.
  - 0 broke off between 390V and 435V.
  - **26 subjects (65%) fully obeyed to 450 volts (Maximum shock)**.
  - 14 subjects (35%) were defiant.
- **Signs of Extreme Tension**:
  - Sweating, trembling, stuttering, groaning, biting lips, digging fingernails into flesh.
  - 14 of 40 subjects exhibited nervous laughing fits and smiling; 3 had full-blown, uncontrollable seizures.
- **Manipulation Check**: Perceived pain on a 14-point scale: Mean = 13.42, Mode = 14 ("Extremely painful").

### Discussion
13 specific features identified by Milgram explaining high obedience, including: Yale prestige, perceived scientific importance, perceived voluntary submission of victim, contractual commitment of subject, lack of clear alternatives, and conflict between two deeply ingrained dispositions (not harming others vs obeying legitimate authority).
