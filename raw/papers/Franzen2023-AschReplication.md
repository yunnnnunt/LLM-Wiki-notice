# The power of social influence: A replication and extension of the Asch experiment

**Authors**: Axel Franzen, Sebastian Mader (University of Bern, Switzerland)  
**Journal**: PLOS ONE, Vol. 18, No. 11, 2023, e0294325.  
**DOI**: 10.1371/journal.pone.0294325  
**Data Availability**: University of Bern repository (https://boris.unibe.ch/id/eprint/169645)  

---

## Abstract
In this paper, we pursue four goals: First, we replicate the original Asch experiment with five confederates and one naïve subject in each group (N = 210). Second, in a randomized trial we incentivize the decisions in the line experiment and demonstrate that monetary incentives lower the error rate, but that social influence is still at work. Third, we confront subjects with different political statements and show that the power of social influence can be generalized to matters of political opinion. Finally, we investigate whether intelligence, self-esteem, the need for social approval, and the Big Five are related to the susceptibility to provide conforming answers. We find an error rate of 33% for the standard length-of-line experiment which replicates the original findings by Asch (1951, 1955, 1956). Furthermore, in the incentivized condition the error rate decreases to 25%. For political opinions we find a conformity rate of 38%. However, besides openness, none of the investigated personality traits are convincingly related to the susceptibility of group pressure.

## Key Highlights & Full Data Points
- **Sample**: 210 university students recruited at University of Bern (female = 61%, mean age = 22.6). Valid analytical sample N = 202 after excluding 4 failed attention checks, 3 suspicious of confederates, 1 non-binary gender.
- **Goal 1 (Replication of Asch)**: 10 line tasks, 4 uncritical/easy (correct), 6 critical trials where 5 confederates give unanimous wrong answers. Non-incentivized error rate = 33%, replicating Asch (1955: 36.8%, t(16) = 0.59, p = 0.57).
- **Goal 2 (Monetary Incentives)**: 102 non-incentivized vs 108 incentivized (20 CHF base + 1 CHF per correct answer up to 30 CHF). Incentives dropped errors from 1.97 to 1.47 (diff = -0.5 errors, t(208) = 2.24, p = 0.03; error rate 33% -> 25%). Group pressure remains strong.
- **Goal 3 (Political Opinions)**: 5 statements (Government power, trade unions, employers' association, citizen liberties, company freedom). Group conformity rate averaged 38 percentage points (p < 0.001 across all 5 statements).
- **Goal 4 (Individual Differences)**:
  - Big Five (BFI-10): Only Openness significantly reduced conformity (beta = -0.30, p < 0.05). Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Neuroticism were non-significant.
  - Rosenberg Self-Esteem (10 items): No significant effect.
  - Hagen Matrices Test (HMT-S, 6 items): Intelligence reduced conformity in men (beta = -0.27), but interaction with gender not significant.
  - Martin Larson Approval Motivation Scale (MLAM, 10 items): Social approval seeking increased conformity in women (chi2(1) = 4.23, p = 0.04), but not in men.
