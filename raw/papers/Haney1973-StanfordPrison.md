# Study of prisoners and guards in a simulated prison

**Authors**: Craig Haney, Curtis Banks, Philip Zimbardo (Stanford University)  
**Journal**: Naval Research Reviews, 1973, 9, 1-17. (Also documented in International Journal of Criminology and Penology, 1973, 1, 69-97)  
**Title**: Interpersonal Dynamics in a Simulated Prison  

---

## Abstract
A simulation study of the psychological effects of imprisonment was conducted on 24 healthy, psychologically normal male college students randomly assigned to play the role of either prisoner or guard in a mock prison in the basement of Stanford University. The planned two-week study had to be halted prematurely after only six days due to severe emotional breakdowns, severe depression, rage, acute anxiety, and abusive behavior exhibited by the guards. Deindividuation, dehumanization, and institutional role internalization overwhelmed moral compasses, demonstrating the supremacy of systemic and situational forces over individual dispositions.

---

## Key Highlights & Full Data Points
- **Sample**: 24 male college students recruited via newspaper ad ($15/day). Thoroughly screened for psychological stability, physical health, and criminal history. Randomly divided into 12 prisoners and 12 guards.
- **Experimental Setting**: Stanford psychology building basement converted into a realistic mock prison with barred cells, solitary confinement ("the Hole"), uniform apparel (prison smocks with ID numbers, stocking caps, chain on ankle vs khaki guard uniforms, silver-plated sunglasses, wooden batons).
- **Core Findings**:
  - Guards rapidly escalated authoritarian and sadistic behavior without explicit instructions (arbitrary push-ups, solitary confinement, verbal degradation, waking prisoners at night).
  - Prisoners became submissive, pathologically depressed, and passive ("pathological prisoner syndrome").
  - On Day 2, prisoners revolted; guards crushed the rebellion with fire extinguishers and increased psychological humiliation.
  - 5 prisoners suffered acute anxiety, uncontrollable crying, and psychosomatic rashes, requiring early release.
  - Experiment was prematurely terminated on Day 6 (originally planned for 14 days) after outside observer Christina Maslach raised moral objections.
- **Methodological and Theoretical Mechanisms**:
  - Deindividuation (loss of individual identity through uniforms, numbers, reflective sunglasses).
  - Systemic legitimacy and institutional permission structure (Lucifer Effect precursor).
